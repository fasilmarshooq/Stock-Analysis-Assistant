# Investment Strategy

## Philosophy

We are value investors, inspired by the principles of Benjamin Graham and Warren Buffett. Our focus is on identifying fundamentally strong companies trading at reasonable valuations, and holding them for the long term. We believe in the power of compounding, patience, and disciplined investing.

## Core Principles

- **Value Investing:** Buy businesses, not just stocks. Focus on intrinsic value, margin of safety, and long-term growth.
- **Rupee Cost Averaging:** Invest a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of market conditions, to average out the purchase price and reduce the impact of volatility.
- **Coffee Can Approach:** Select high-quality companies and hold them for extended periods, minimizing churn and transaction costs.
- **Budgeted Allocation:** Set a maximum investment budget for each stock (scrip) and deploy capital gradually.

## Buying Strategy

1. **Budget Allocation:**  
   - Assign a fixed investment budget for each stock.  
   - Example: For ITC, the total budget is ₹1,00,000.

2. **Gradual Deployment:**  
   - Invest in tranches, not all at once.
   - Example: If ₹20,000 is already invested in ITC, ₹80,000 remains to be deployed over time.

3. **Periodic Purchases:**  
   - Buy small quantities at regular intervals (e.g., monthly or quarterly), regardless of short-term price movements.
   - This implements rupee cost averaging and reduces the risk of poor market timing.

4. **Valuation and Fundamentals:**  
   - Prefer stocks with strong fundamentals, even if the current P/E is high, provided the business quality and long-term prospects are intact.
   - Monitor key metrics: current price, 52-week high/low, P/E ratio, and business performance.

5. **Example: ITC**  
   - **Budget:** ₹1,00,000  
   - **Invested so far:** ₹20,000  
   - **Current Price:** ₹414  
   - **52-Week High:** ₹495  
   - **P/E Ratio:** 25.9  
   - **Strategy:** Despite a relatively high P/E and proximity to 52-week high, ITC’s strong fundamentals justify continued, gradual accumulation. We will buy 1-2 shares periodically, staying within our budget, and hold for the long term.

## Opportunistic Buying

While we follow a disciplined, budgeted approach to investing, we remain open to exceptional opportunities. If a stock we already own experiences a significant price drop due to market sentiment, temporary events, or broader market corrections—yet the company’s fundamentals remain strong—we may consider additional purchases even if our original budget for that stock has been fully deployed.

**Guidelines for Opportunistic Buying:**
- Ensure the price drop is not due to a fundamental deterioration in the business.
- Reassess the company’s intrinsic value and long-term prospects.
- Only consider additional investment if the stock offers a substantial margin of safety.
- Avoid over-concentration in any single stock; maintain overall portfolio balance.

This approach allows us to take advantage of rare market dislocations while staying true to our value investing principles.

## Goals

- Fully deploy the allocated budget for each stock over time, without rushing purchases.
- Build a portfolio of high-quality businesses, held for the long term.
- Minimize emotional decision-making by sticking to a systematic, rules-based approach.

## Review and Rebalancing

- Review portfolio and individual stock performance annually.
- Rebalance only if fundamentals deteriorate or if a stock becomes significantly overvalued relative to its intrinsic worth.

---

**Note:** This strategy is designed for disciplined, long-term wealth creation. It is not intended for short-term trading or speculation.
